General notice. **FR Doc 2026-16911**, **91 FR 53627**, published
2026-08-19. It is CBP's first published step implementing
[EO 14411](eo-14411.md) §2(e), which requires confirming that active IORs
comply with all applicable regulations and disclosures.

From **2026-09-18**, CBP **immediately voids** any IOR number whose CBP Form
5106 data it finds inaccurate or incomplete. A voided number is invalid for
any purpose, including making entry. CBP is reviewing all 5106 data on file,
not just new filings. The data must belong to the IOR itself:

- **Physical address:** the IOR's actual location. It cannot be a
  registered agent, customs broker, freight forwarder, P.O. box, business
  service center, or another party's address. For an individual, a home
  address is acceptable.
- **Email and phone:** valid and the IOR's own. Brokers and third parties
  may not substitute their own contact details.
- **EIN/SSN:** must belong directly to the IOR.
- **Power of attorney:** a broker filing a 5106 must hold a POA executed
  **directly with the IOR**, not through a freight forwarder or other third
  party (19 CFR 111.36(c)(3)). Brokers must exercise due diligence and must
  not transmit information they know, or should know, is false or
  unverified (19 CFR 111.29(a), 111.32).
- **Exposure:** false statements fall under 18 U.S.C. 1001. Inaccurate IOR
  data is material to duty liability, so the False Claims Act can apply.
  Brokers also face penalties under 19 U.S.C. 1641.

**Process:** CBP emails the voiding notice to the IOR's most recent 5106
email address, copying the last filing broker, with instructions for
reestablishment. Requests go to IORProgram@cbp.dhs.gov with the subject
"Enforcing IOR Accuracy".
