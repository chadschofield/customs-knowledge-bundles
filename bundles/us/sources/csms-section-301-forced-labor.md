CBP filing guidance for the **USTR Section 301 forced-labor action** (Trade
Act of 1974), announced **2026-07-23** and effective for goods entered for
consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption, on or after
**2026-07-24 12:01 a.m. eastern standard time**: additional ad valorem duties
of **10% or 12.5%** on imports from **60 economies**, declared via Chapter 99
headings **9903.05.20–9903.05.84**.

This is the Forced Labor Investigation Program (FLIP) action — the first time
forced labor is used as a **tariff** lever rather than only an admissibility
bar (see [Forced Labor Enforcement](/enforcement/forced-labor.md)).

# Rate Structure

- **12.5%** for most covered economies (Algeria, Angola, Australia, Bahamas,
  Bahrain, Brazil, Chile, China, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic,
  Egypt, Guyana, Hong Kong, Iraq, Israel, Kazakhstan, Kuwait, Libya, Morocco,
  New Zealand, Nicaragua, Nigeria, Norway, Oman, Peru, Philippines, Qatar,
  Russia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, Thailand, Türkiye, UAE,
  Uruguay, Venezuela, Vietnam).
- **10%** for Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador,
  Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan,
  Sri Lanka, Trinidad and Tobago, United Kingdom.
- **Threshold pairs** for the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, and Taiwan:
  a first heading applies **no additional duty** where the column-one rate
  already meets the threshold (10% or 12.5% depending on the economy), and a
  second heading brings the goods up to a **combined** rate where it does not
  — e.g. EU 9903.05.38 (column one ≥10%, no additional duty) vs 9903.05.39
  (combined 10%); Japan 9903.05.48/.49 at the 12.5% threshold.

# Exemptions

General, applying across all covered economies (**9903.05.85–9903.05.92**):

- **In-transit window (9903.05.85):** goods loaded at the port of loading and
  in transit on the final mode before 2026-07-24 12:01 a.m. ET *and* entered
  before **2026-07-28**.
- **U.S. note 52(b)–(f) carve-outs (9903.05.86–.90):** including civil
  aircraft articles, engines, parts and flight simulators (52(d)); articles
  for use in pharmaceutical applications (52(e)); and goods already covered by
  other trade-remedy programs — aluminum, steel and copper articles,
  passenger and light-duty vehicles, medium/heavy-duty vehicles, wood
  products, and semiconductor articles (52(f)), which keeps this action from
  stacking on [Section 232](/tariff/tariff-actions-2025-2026.md).
- **Donations (9903.05.91)** and **informational materials (9903.05.92)**.

Economy-specific exemptions ride **9903.05.93–9903.05.99** and
**9903.06.01–9903.06.21**, keyed to U.S. note 52(g)–(j) — notably USMCA
free-entry products of Canada (9903.05.93) and Mexico (9903.05.94), and
CAFTA-DR duty-free textiles and apparel (9903.05.95).

The covered Chapter 1–97 classifications corresponding to each Chapter 99
heading are in the attached list.

The corresponding ACE tariff records loaded via **HSU 2617** (created
