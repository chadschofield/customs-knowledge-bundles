Advance notice of proposed rulemaking. **FR Doc 2026-17926**, **91 FR
56408**, published 2026-09-02. **Comments are due 2026-12-01**; questions go
to supplychainvisibility@cbp.dhs.gov. It implements **§3 of
[EO 14411](eo-14411.md)** (heightened import disclosures). It proposes no
regulatory text. It sets out CBP's options and asks **64 questions**, and the
comments will shape a later NPRM. CBP also asks whether to phase any
requirement by entry type, commodity, country or mode, and whether small
entities, foreign importers, CTPAT members or high-volume filers should get
different timelines.

The three proposal areas:

- **Foreign export documentation (Q1–23).** The importer would submit or
  keep what the foreign exporter filed with its own customs authority:
  export declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of
  origin, export licence, and transport document. The aim is to expose
  dual invoicing and other discrepancies. Open questions include filing at
  entry versus recordkeeping, random versus blanket requests, retention
  period, translation, verification with foreign customs, CTPAT
  differences, and mandatory submission for goods designated a "grave risk"
  to national security.
- **Party identification (Q24–45).**
  - Whether to **redefine or replace the MID** with full manufacturer,
    shipper and exporter identity, possibly at entry and on the manifest.
  - Whether to make **Global Business Identifiers** (D-U-N-S, GLN, LEI,
    Altana ID; currently a voluntary test) or **foreign tax IDs**
    mandatory, or to collect product-level identifiers.
  - Whether to identify the **online marketplace** that facilitated the sale
    (Q33) and the **party the goods will finally be delivered to** (Q34).
  - Whether **entry should be filed earlier** (Q43).
- **Supply-chain tracing technology (Q46 onward):** novel tracing
  solutions, plus questions on economic impact.
