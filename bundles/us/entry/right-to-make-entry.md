Section 484 of the Tariff Act (19 U.S.C. 1484) restricts who may make entry:
only the **importer of record** — defined as the **owner or purchaser** of the
goods, or a **licensed customs broker** designated by the owner, purchaser, or
consignee. Customs Directive 3530-002A (2001) is CBP's standing guidance on
this rule.

# Core Rules (Directive 3530-002A)

- A **nominal consignee** (e.g. a carrier, forwarder, or consolidator named on
  shipping documents) may **designate** a licensed broker to make entry, but
  may not make entry itself. When a broker enters for a nominal consignee, the
  **broker must appear as importer of record**.
- The 1982 amendment behind the rule (Pub. L. 97-446) exists to stop parties
  without a license or ownership interest from transacting customs business.
- Conducting customs business for others without a broker license is
  sanctionable (19 U.S.C. 1641).

# Why This Bites in the Post-De-Minimis World

| Context | Consequence |
|---------|-------------|
| [Postal IMDW filing](/entry/postal-informal-entry-imdw.md) (from 2026-07-24) | Filer must have the right to make entry; in practice a **licensed US broker** — non-broker "Qualified Parties" are out. Foreign postal operators, USPS, forwarders, and carriers must engage one. |
| [Entry Type 13 test](/entry/entry-type-13-test.md) (from 2026-09-22) | Consignee-participants (FPOs, USPS, forwarders, carriers) must appoint a licensed broker who acts as **IOR**, obligating the broker's bond. |
| Non-postal [Entry Type 11](/entry/entry-types.md) | Owner, purchaser, or broker files — same as always, but volumes that previously cleared off the manifest now need a filer with entry rights. |
| [EO 14411](/enforcement/eo-14411-enforcement.md) (from 2026-11-30) | **Foreign IORs are prohibited from informal entry** entirely, face asset/bonding minimums, and need CTPAT validation (their own or their broker's) for formal entry. |

An importer must also have a CBP Form 5106 importer identity record in ACE;
brokers file against it, and [EO 14411](/enforcement/eo-14411-enforcement.md)
directs CBP to harden this registry with vetting and beneficial-ownership
disclosure.
