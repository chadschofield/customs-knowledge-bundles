Goods made wholly or in part with forced labor are **inadmissible** to the
United States. This sits alongside the importer-accountability push in
[EO 14411](/enforcement/eo-14411-enforcement.md) — both raise the diligence
burden on importers of record and their supply chains. For apparel,
electronics, and cross-border ecommerce, it is a live detention risk, not a
theoretical one.

# The Three Authorities

| Authority | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|
| **19 U.S.C. § 1307** | Blanket prohibition on importing goods mined, produced, or manufactured with forced/convict/indentured labor. Enforced through **Withhold Release Orders (WROs)** and **Findings**. |
| **UFLPA** (Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act) | **Rebuttable presumption** that goods with any nexus to Xinjiang (XUAR) or the UFLPA Entity List are made with forced labor and barred, unless the importer rebuts by clear and convincing evidence. |
| **CAATSA** (Pub. L. 115-44) | Presumption barring goods produced with North Korean labor anywhere in the supply chain. |

# WROs and Findings

- A **WRO** directs ports to **detain** shipments when information reasonably
  indicates forced labor; the importer must export the goods or prove
  admissibility. A **Finding** is a stronger determination allowing seizure.
- WROs are issued against specific producers/merchandise and take effect
  immediately. Example: on **2026-06-23** CBP issued WROs against garments
  from **Needle Craft Ltd.** and **Casual Wear Apparel L.L.C.** in Jordan
  (CSMS # 69031301) — the kind of apparel-sector action that reaches
  ecommerce supply chains.

# CBP's Operational Guidance (2026)

CBP consolidated all three authorities into a single **Forced Labor
Enforcement Operational Guidance for Importers** (launched 2026-06-12,
CSMS # 68927213), which adds:

- process maps for **UFLPA, WRO, Finding, and CAATSA** actions;
- step-by-step sections on responding to **detentions and exclusions**;
- appendices on recommended supply-chain documentation for high-priority
  sectors, UFLPA due-diligence examples, and sample detention/exclusion and
  redelivery notices.

Importers are expected to conduct supplier due diligence **before** importing.

# Practical Reading

- Country-of-origin and manufacturer-identity accuracy (the
  [5106/MID](/ace-filing/importer-manufacturer-ids.md) data) is what CBP
  matches against WRO/UFLPA targets — misdeclaration compounds the exposure.
- A detention converts a low-margin parcel flow into a documentation burden;
  factor forced-labor screening into onboarding for apparel and
  electronics sellers.
