**Executive Order 14411 — "Strengthening Customs Enforcement"** (signed
2026-06-03; FR Doc 2026-11595) directs DHS/CBP and DOJ to overhaul importer
vetting, penalties, and supply-chain transparency, aimed at undervaluation,
evasion, forced labor, counterfeits, and synthetic-drug smuggling. The order
sets deadlines for agencies to issue implementing rules — the operative detail
arrives via rulemakings to watch for.

# IOR Definitions (Section 10)

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **U.S. IOR** | A US citizen or lawful permanent resident; or an entity organized under US law, **located in the US** (principal place of business + physical presence with significant business activity + sufficient tangible US assets), with controlling beneficial owners who are citizens/LPRs or significant US real property. |
| **Foreign IOR** | Any importer of record not meeting the U.S. IOR definition. |

# Mandates and Deadlines

| Deadline | Mandate |
|----------|---------|
| **2026-07-18** (45 days) | DHS/OMB legislative recommendations to the President. |
| **2026-09-01** (90 days) | • **50% minimum penalty floor** (mitigation cannot reduce below 50% of the assessed penalty absent exceptional national-security circumstances) • **No mitigation for repeat offenders** • Maximum penalties for brokers failing due diligence or repeatedly representing noncompliant clients • **Foreign-exporter documentation**: importers must submit what the exporter filed with its own customs authority • Expedited seizure/disposal (19 U.S.C. 1612; voluntary abandonment) • Enhanced transparency rules. |
| **2026-11-30** (180 days) | • **Foreign IORs prohibited from informal entries** (19 U.S.C. 1498) — includes [Entry Type 11](/entry/entry-types.md) • Foreign IORs may not use continuous bonds for formal entry unless CBP authorizes • Foreign IORs must be **CTPAT-validated or use a CTPAT-validated licensed US broker** • Minimum tangible-asset and [bonding](/entry/bonds.md) levels • IOR registry update + enhanced vetting. |
| **2027-06-03** (1 year) | DHS effectiveness report. |

# Disclosure & Certification Requirements (90-day track)

- Beneficial ownership structures, business affiliations, year organized,
  anticipated import volumes
- Foreign tax and global business identifiers
- Supply-chain data (manufacturer product identifier, composition, grade/size)
- Certification of compliance with **CAATSA** (Pub. L. 115-44) and
  **18 U.S.C. 545** (smuggling)
- **"Good standing" rule**: importers found to have imported fentanyl,
  nitazenes, or precursor chemicals are barred from importing.

# Already in Effect

- **IOR deactivation (2026):** CBP deactivates dormant IOR numbers, and since
  **2026-07-16** ACE does it automatically — no entry filed within 366 days
  (19 CFR 24.5(e)) flips the account to "Inactive for Entry Purposes"
  (CSMS # 69056621, # 69241265) — the enforcement clean-up ahead of the
  registry overhaul. Reactivation process and the reject codes it triggers
  are in [Right to Make Entry](/entry/right-to-make-entry.md).
- **Forced labor:** CBP's consolidated
  [forced-labor enforcement guidance](/enforcement/forced-labor.md)
  (19 U.S.C. 1307 / UFLPA / CAATSA) operationalizes the supply-chain
  diligence this order presses on importers.

# Practical Reading for Parcel Logistics

- Foreign merchant-of-record models that self-file informal entries stop
  working 2026-11-30 — route through US entities or brokers with
  [entry rights](/entry/right-to-make-entry.md).
- Broker liability rises (penalty exposure for client noncompliance), which
  will tighten broker onboarding/KYC for shippers.
- Expect the implementing rulemakings to add commercial-entry data elements
  (the 2026 non-postal IFR deliberately added none, deferring to this EO).
